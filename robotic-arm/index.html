01Overview

A desktop six-axis arm designed in Inventor around off-the-shelf actuators: NEMA17 steppers with StepperOnline planetary gearboxes at the base, shoulder and elbow, and DSSERVO digital servos at the wrist and gripper. I designed every reduction stage myself: printed involute spur gears on J1, J2, J4 and J5, and an HTD 5M belt inside the box-section upper arm for J3. The belt puts the elbow motor next to the shoulder, which takes torque off J2.

Joint angles are Inventor parameters that drive a fully constrained assembly, and each gear pinion is tied to its joint so the teeth stay in mesh at any pose. All structural parts and gears are designed to print in PA6-CF on a Bambu printer.

A later buildability check against the real gearbox ratings found the 0.5 kg payload marginal at the shoulder once acceleration is included, and both PG19 output shafts overloaded sideways (the 100 N rating). I kept the 0.5 kg payload and respecced the drives: a higher shoulder ratio, jackshafts in their own bearings so the gearboxes carry torque only, wider wrist gears, slotted gearbox mounts and D-bored pinions. The model, drawings, BOM and calculations were rebuilt; the arm has not been built.

Tools Autodesk Inventor 2027 / Python / scikit-fem / gmsh / NumPy / Matplotlib / Bambu Studio

What I did

  • Sized every joint from a Newton-Euler model built on the CAD mass properties. A grid search over the joint ranges found the worst static torque, and I checked each motor, gearbox and gear stage for torque, speed and gearbox rating.
  • Modelled involute gears in Inventor and coupled each pinion to its joint through the ratio and mesh phase; interference stays at zero over a full tooth pitch on every pair.
  • Validated the kinematics by driving Inventor's solver to random poses and comparing it with my Python forward kinematics, then swept every joint with interference checks to set collision-free limits.
  • Ran gmsh + scikit-fem FEA on the critical printed parts. The DfAM check showed the horn bolt holes cutting into the pinion roots and high gear tooth stress, so I redesigned the gears.

02Design decisions7 changes

  1. 01

    Belt-driven elbow to unload the shoulder

    Finding
    The shoulder (J2) is the most heavily loaded joint: 6.54 N.m worst static torque at full extension against 13.3 N.m of continuous capacity. A motor mounted at the elbow would add its own mass to the end of that lever.
    Action
    Drove the elbow (J3) through an HTD 5M belt inside the box-section upper arm, so the elbow motor sits next to the shoulder instead of out on the arm.
    Outcome
    The belt stage gives J3 a 57.6:1 total ratio with a 137 N tight-side load (SF 2.19); J2 now has a static SF of 2.30 after the respec.
  2. 02

    Gears redesigned after the DfAM check

    Finding
    The design-for-printing check showed the servo-horn bolt holes cutting into the pinion roots, and tooth bending stress was the weak point of the printed gears.
    Action
    Redesigned the gears and re-checked every pair with the Lewis equation and Barth velocity factor against a 30 MPa allowable for printed PA6-CF.
    Outcome
    After the respec the lowest tooth safety factor is 1.68 (J1); J2 2.45, J4 2.78, J5 2.61.
  3. 03

    Pinions tied to their joints

    Finding
    Joint angles are parameters that drive the whole assembly, so free-floating gears would drift out of mesh as the arm moves.
    Action
    Coupled each pinion to its joint in Inventor through the gear ratio and the mesh phase.
    Outcome
    Zero interference over a full tooth pitch on every gear pair.
  4. 04

    Joint limits set by interference sweeps

    Finding
    Datasheet ranges say nothing about where the printed links hit each other.
    Action
    Swept every joint through its range in Inventor with interference checks at fixed poses of the other joints.
    Outcome
    Collision-free limits: J1 ±170 deg, J2 -60 to 110 deg, J3 ±100 deg, J4 and J6 ±180 deg, J5 -60 to 100 deg.
  5. 05

    Buildability check: gearbox shafts and wrist gears

    Finding
    My first analysis checked torque but not the gearbox shaft side-load rating. The J3 belt pulls the PG19 output shaft sideways with 117-130 N against a 100 N rating. With acceleration included, J2 needs 7.06 N.m, which leaves the gearbox at SF 1.88 with 107 N of side load. The printed J4/J5 wrist gears would strip if a servo stalled (107-116 MPa tooth stress against 102 MPa UTS).
    Action
    Decision: keep the 0.5 kg payload rather than derate to 0.3 kg. Side load scales as T_joint / (eta r_gear cos 20), so no realistic ratio change fixes it: the J2 pinion and the J3 pulley now ride on 8 mm jackshafts in two 608 bearings each, driven through Ruland MJC25 jaw couplings (8.48 N.m) on printed stand-offs, so the gearboxes carry torque only. J2 went from 18:84 to 16:86 at the same 89.25 mm centres; J3 belt tension moved to eccentric 608 housings.
    Outcome
    Gearbox SF with acceleration 1.88 -> 2.14 (SF 2 holds up to 0.58 kg); gearbox side load 107-130 N -> torque only (jackshaft 608s at 50-65 N, s0 21 or more; couplings SF 6-8).
  6. 06

    Wrist gears that survive a stalled servo

    Finding
    At a 7.4 V stall the printed J4 and J5 pinions see 116 and 107 MPa against a 102 MPa UTS, and J4 ran at a working SF of 1.22.
    Action
    Run the servos at 6 V, widen the J4 gears from 8 to 14 mm (servo moved back 6 mm), and change J5 from m1.0 20:40 to m1.25 16:32 at the same 30 mm centres.
    Outcome
    Stall stress 60.6 MPa (J4, SF 1.68) and 84.8 MPa (J5, SF 1.20); working tooth SF 2.13 / 2.03.
  7. 07

    Interfaces to real parts, and the clashes the rebuild found

    Finding
    The PG19 bolt circle was assumed (30 mm; the usual 36 mm NEMA17 planetary is 28 mm) and the pinions had plain round bores. Rebuilding the assembly then showed the stand-off gearbox screw heads inside the tube bore and the bigger 86T gear clipping a cheek foot screw.
    Action
    Slotted every gearbox mount for a 28-30 mm bolt circle, D-bored the J1/J2 pinions, opened the stand-off tubes to 34.5/35 mm and moved the foot screws 6 mm out.
    Outcome
    111 parts, 0 DOF, 0 clashes at 5 poses apart from one explained overlap (the J1 D-bore on the round shaft of the one-piece motor model); the joint ranges from the interference sweep are unchanged.

P5 What else you need to make it work

From Tests/Buildability_Audit/buildability_audit.md (2026-10-28). Verdict: BUILDABLE WITH FIXES.

Also needed

Firmware
Stepper + servo control with a homing routine and soft position limits; close the gripper to object width minus about 2 mm, not to the hard stop.
Test first
Print a bearing-seat coupon first: printed seats for the 6004 / 6201 / 6806 / 608 usually need +0.1-0.2 mm.
Printing
PA6-CF needs a hardened nozzle, dried filament and an enclosed printer. UpperArmA / B (274 mm) only fit a 256 mm bed on the diagonal. The J3 drive mount needs tree supports under its flange (9 plates).
Use
The arm falls when the drivers switch off: keep them enabled and park the arm before powering down.
Respec done
2026-11-01, payload kept at 0.5 kg: J2 16T:86T (gearbox SF 2.14), J2 pinion and J3 pulley on jackshafts in 608s with Ruland MJC25 couplings (PG19 shafts carry torque only), J4 face 14 / J5 m1.25, servos at 6 V, gearbox mounts slotted for PCD 28-30, D-bored pinions. The FEA was not re-run after the respec.
Measure first
The PG19 front bolt circle (mounts slotted for 28-30 mm) and the coupling hub fit on the 8 mm D-cut shafts.

Now in the BOM: Stepper drivers + controller; Servo PWM driver; 24 V 150 W power supply; Servo supply; 3 homing sensors; Wiring; DIN 471-20 retaining ring for the J1 shaft; 16 x M2 horn screws; 5 or more M4 heat-set inserts for the insert holes in UpperArmB; Silicone / rubber gripper pads; Filament.

Cost in the buildability file: BOM $703.34 after the 2026-11-01 respec ($303.56 confirmed on vendor pages, $399.78 estimated incl. two Ruland jaw couplings).

09Limits and assumptionsread before quoting a number

Everything here is calculated or simulated from the CAD model; the arm has not been built. The FEA pictures and the motion study are from the first design: the respec added bearing bosses and moved the motor weights about 50 mm outboard, and the FEA was not re-run. The coupling price and the PG19 bolt circle are not confirmed (the mounts are slotted for 28-30 mm). The main assumptions are a stepper pull-out curve typical of the motor class, 50% of servo stall torque as the continuous rating, a 30 MPa allowable stress for printed gear teeth, a 300 N belt working limit, solid printed parts in the FEA, and estimated motor and gearbox masses. The calculation report lists each one.
