01Overview

A rear drivetrain for a 185 kg electric kart (driver included) that has to reach 25 mph, designed in Inventor from one design file so the CAD, calculations and FEA share the same numbers. A Motenergy ME1114 brushless motor on a 350 A, 48 V controller drives a 1-1/4 in 4140 live axle through a single ANSI #35 chain stage (13/60 teeth, 4.615:1). I picked the ratio from the motor's back-EMF: 25 mph lands at 3595 rpm, so the kart still reaches 25 mph on a nearly flat 16S LiFePO4 pack.

The motor sits on a machined 6061-T6 bracket with four tensioning slots, and the axle runs in UC206 insert bearings on 4130 hangers welded to the chassis rails. Two Inventor parameters drive the assembly: ChainLine keeps the sprockets coplanar, and ChainAdjust slides the motor to tension the chain. The FEA led to a redesign of both mounts.

It covers the drivetrain only: there is no brake in the CAD or BOM yet, and a later buildability check found the #35 chain overloaded at launch. Both are listed under design decisions. Nothing has been built.

Tools Autodesk Inventor 2027 / Python / NumPy / SciPy / scikit-fem / gmsh / Matplotlib

What I did

  • Sized the gearing from the motor's back-EMF: 13/60 puts 25 mph at 3595 rpm, so the kart still makes 25 mph on a nearly flat pack while the controller governs it on a full one.
  • Wrote a longitudinal launch model with the controller, battery-sag and voltage limits, rotating inertia and traction with load transfer. It gives 0-25 mph in about 2 s, gradeability and range.
  • Closed the 98-link chain loop exactly and phased both sprockets' teeth to its pitch points, so the modelled chain sits in the gaps. Two Inventor parameters keep the sprockets coplanar and slide the motor in its tensioning slots, both verified with interference sweeps.
  • Used gmsh + scikit-fem FEA on the motor bracket, bearing hanger and axle under launch, 2.5 g bump, 1.5 g cornering and kerb-strike cases; the results drove a redesign of both mounts.

02Design decisions2 changes

  1. 01

    Bearing hanger and motor bracket resized by FEA

    Finding
    The first 1/4 in bearing hanger reached SF 1.75 with 3.0 mm deflection in the 2 g kerb + 1 g side case, while the motor bracket was far stronger than it needed to be.
    Action
    Went to a 3/8 in hanger with blended corners and took material out of the bracket.
    Outcome
    Hanger SF 3.57 and 0.9 mm deflection; the bracket lost 0.55 kg and sits at SF 6.5.
  2. 02

    Buildability check: chain, sprocket, brake

    Finding
    My chain check used ultimate strength only. Against the #35 working allowable (2135 N) the chain sees 99-126% at launch. The 13T #35 sprocket with a 7/8 in bore is not a catalogue part, and there is no brake anywhere in the design; it needs about 127 N.m at the rear axle.
    Action
    Fixes identified: a Browning H3515X7/8 15T sprocket (4.00:1, 100-link chain) with a 350 A current limit, or #35-2 duplex / #40 chain to use the full 450 A; add a rear disc brake. Also to correct: the rim is too narrow for 11x7.10-5 tyres, and the UCFL206 bolt holes are 16 mm, not 14.
    Outcome
    Open: the model has not been changed yet.

From Tests/Buildability_Audit/buildability_audit.md (2026-10-28). Verdict: BUILDABLE WITH FIXES.

Missing from the BOM

  • Blocker

    Brake: 1-1/4 in brake-disc hub, 180-200 mm disc, hydraulic kart caliper and master cylinder (needs about 127 N.m of axle brake torque). There is no brake anywhere in the design. ~USD 150-300 (est.)

  • Missing

    Sheet-metal chain and sprocket guard ~USD 20-40 (est.)

Change before buying

  1. Motor sprocket GK-401 (13T #35, 7/8 bore) is not a catalogue part. Use a Browning H3515X7/8 (15T): ratio 4.00, and the chain goes to 100 links.
  2. Chain GK-403 runs at 99 % of its allowable load at 350 A (126 % at the controller's full 450 A). Limit the controller to 300-350 A with the 15T sprocket, or go to #35-2 duplex / #40.
  3. 60T sprocket GK-402: no #35 60T with the 6-bolt 3.25 in pattern was found. Buy a Martin 35BS60 bored 1-1/4 with a keyway (this deletes the GK-302 hub), or a 5-1/4 in BC split sprocket.
  4. Wheels GK-411 / GK-412: 11x7.10-5 tyres need 7.75-8.00 in wide rims (CAD 5 in), and real 1-1/4 in kart hubs are 3-bolt on 2.5 in (e.g. JOES 25560). Buy hub and rim as a pair.
  5. Battery GK-602: no off-the-shelf 40 Ah / 250 A pack was found. Build it from 16 cells with a 16S BMS rated 250 A or more, or accept about 2.8 s to 25 mph with a 150 A BMS.
  6. Bearing units GK-421: the real UCFL206 holes are 16 mm (CAD 14). The 1/2-13 bolts work, but add hardened washers.

Also needed

Measure first
Get the Motenergy ME1114 mechanical drawing before machining the motor bracket GK-103: the face bolt pattern is assumed.
Controller
The Sevcon Gen4 needs programming (DVT software) and a harness; set the phase-current limit to protect the chain.
Assembly
Clock the UC insert set screws at least 90 deg away from the axle keyway. A ready-keyed 1-1/4 x 40 in kart axle avoids milling a 1016 mm keyway.
Not included
Chassis, steering, seat and shop time are not part of this project or its cost.

09Limits and assumptionsread before quoting a number

Designed and simulated only: the drivetrain has not been built, and every number is a model prediction. There is no brake in the design, so it is not road- or track-ready as drawn. Motor data comes from the Motenergy ME1114 listing; the rotor inertia, face bolt pattern and outer shape are assumed. The controller and battery are class assumptions and are not modelled in CAD. The kart mass, CG, rolling resistance, CdA and tyre friction are typical assumed values. Loads are quasi-static limit cases. Sprockets, chain, bearings, hubs, rims and tyres are catalogue-dimension models; the fasteners are vendor STEP files. FEA is linear elastic without weld fillets or keys, and both peak and 99.9th-percentile safety factors are quoted. Every made part is steel or aluminium, so there is no 3D-print file.
