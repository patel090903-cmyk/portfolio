01Overview

A rear drivetrain for a 185 kg electric kart (driver included) that has to reach 25 mph, designed in Inventor from one design file so the CAD, calculations and FEA share the same numbers. A Motenergy ME1114 brushless motor on a 350 A, 48 V controller drives a 1-1/4 in 4140 live axle through a single ANSI #40 chain stage (Martin 40BS13 / 40BS50, 3.85:1). The ratio comes from the motor's back-EMF: 25 mph lands at 2996 rpm, so the kart still reaches 25 mph on a nearly flat 16S LiFePO4 pack.

The motor sits on a machined 6061-T6 bracket with four tensioning slots, and the axle runs in UC206 insert bearings on 4130 hangers welded to the chassis rails. Three Inventor parameters drive the assembly: Drive turns the axle, wheels, brake disc and (at 3.85x) the motor shaft and sprocket; ChainLine keeps the sprockets coplanar; ChainAdjust slides the motor to tension the chain. The FEA led to a redesign of both mounts.

A buildability check found the first #35 chain overloaded at launch and no brake anywhere. I respecced it: #40 chain on stock Martin sprockets keyed straight to the axle, plus a hydraulic disc brake on the right of the axle and a chain guard, all in the CAD and the BOM. Nothing has been built.

Tools Autodesk Inventor 2027 / Python / NumPy / SciPy / scikit-fem / gmsh / Matplotlib

What I did

  • Sized the gearing from the motor's back-EMF; after the #40 respec 13/50 puts 25 mph at 2996 rpm, so the kart still makes 25 mph on a nearly flat pack while the controller governs it on a full one.
  • Wrote a longitudinal launch model with the controller, battery-sag and voltage limits, rotating inertia and traction with load transfer. It gives 0-25 mph in about 2 s, gradeability and range.
  • Closed the 78-link chain loop exactly and phased both sprockets' teeth to its pitch points, so the modelled chain sits in the gaps. The Drive parameter turns the axle and the motor shaft at the real 50:13 ratio (measured 0.0 deg error), and the chain still seats exactly at whole-tooth steps; ChainLine and ChainAdjust are verified with interference sweeps.
  • Used gmsh + scikit-fem FEA on the motor bracket, bearing hanger and axle under launch, 2.5 g bump, 1.5 g cornering and kerb-strike cases; the results drove a redesign of both mounts.

02Design decisions2 changes

  1. 01

    Bearing hanger and motor bracket resized by FEA

    Finding
    The first 1/4 in bearing hanger reached SF 1.75 with 3.0 mm deflection in the 2 g kerb + 1 g side case, while the motor bracket was far stronger than it needed to be.
    Action
    Went to a 3/8 in hanger with blended corners and took material out of the bracket.
    Outcome
    Hanger SF 3.57 and 0.9 mm deflection; the bracket lost 0.55 kg and sits at SF 6.5.
  2. 02

    Buildability check: chain, sprocket, brake

    Finding
    My chain check used ultimate strength only. Against the #35 working allowable (2135 N) the chain sees 99-126% at launch. The 13T #35 sprocket with a 7/8 in bore is not a catalogue part, and there is no brake anywhere in the design; it needs about 127 N.m at the rear axle.
    Action
    Fixes identified: a Browning H3515X7/8 15T sprocket (4.00:1, 100-link chain) with a 350 A current limit, or #35-2 duplex / #40 chain to use the full 450 A; add a rear disc brake. Also to correct: the rim is too narrow for 11x7.10-5 tyres, and the UCFL206 bolt holes are 16 mm, not 14.
    Outcome
    Respecced 2026-10-29: #40 chain (78 links) on a Martin 40BS13 7/8 and a 40BS50 1-1/4 keyed straight to the axle (the bolt-on hub and 6 bolts are gone), 3.85:1. Launch tension 1.57 kN = 44% of the #40 allowable (62% at the traction limit). Added a 180 mm hydraulic disc brake (hub, disc, caliper on a bracket welded to the right hanger) and a chain guard, re-checked with 0 clashes. Still open: the rim width, the UCFL206 hole size and a check of the caliper bracket weld.

P4 What else you need to make it work

From Tests/Buildability_Audit/buildability_audit.md (2026-10-28). Verdict: BUILDABLE WITH FIXES.

Change before buying

  1. Brake GK-703 (GoPowerSports Mid S kit, single piston): the vendor does not publish clamp force, disc thickness range or master-cylinder bore, so the ~127 N.m axle brake torque is not verified. Ask the vendor before ordering (no returns on brake parts). from BOM price source (GoKartDrivetrain_BOM.csv)
  2. Caliper bracket GK-704: welded to the right hanger; its weld and the hanger under the brake reaction (~1.6 kN) were not checked. from Tests\Working_Model_Check.md
  3. Wheels GK-411 / GK-412: 11x7.10-5 tyres need 7.75-8.00 in wide rims (CAD 5 in), and real 1-1/4 in kart hubs are 3-bolt on 2.5 in (e.g. JOES 25560). Buy hub and rim as a pair.
  4. Battery GK-602: no off-the-shelf 40 Ah / 250 A pack was found. Build it from 16 cells with a 16S BMS rated 250 A or more, or accept about 2.8 s to 25 mph with a 150 A BMS.
  5. Bearing units GK-421: the real UCFL206 holes are 16 mm (CAD 14). The 1/2-13 bolts work, but add hardened washers.

09Limits and assumptionsread before quoting a number

Designed and simulated only: the drivetrain has not been built, and every number is a model prediction. The brake hub, disc and caliper are catalogue-class envelopes (GoPowerSports listings); the caliper bracket weld is not yet checked. Motor data comes from the Motenergy ME1114 listing; the rotor inertia, face bolt pattern and outer shape are assumed. The controller and battery are class assumptions and are not modelled in CAD. The kart mass, CG, rolling resistance, CdA and tyre friction are typical assumed values. Loads are quasi-static limit cases. Sprockets, chain, bearings, hubs, rims and tyres are catalogue-dimension models; the fasteners are vendor STEP files. FEA is linear elastic without weld fillets or keys, and both peak and 99.9th-percentile safety factors are quoted. Every made part is steel or aluminium, so there is no 3D-print file.
