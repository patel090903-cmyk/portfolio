01Overview

I designed a complete quadcopter airframe in Inventor around a T-Motor AIR2216 / 1045 propulsion set, a Pixhawk 6C flight controller and a 4S 5000 mAh pack. The frame is meant to be printed in PA6-CF on a Bambu printer. Purchased parts are vendor models or sized stand-ins, and every printable part has a CAD-measured mass, an FEA result and a print file. The drone has not been built.

Tools Autodesk Inventor 2027 / Python / scikit-fem / gmsh / SU2 / Bambu Studio

What I did

  • Wrote a 6-DOF flight simulator in Python (quaternion dynamics, inertia from the CAD, blade-element rotor model) and closed the loop with a PX4-style cascaded PID controller.
  • Checked the rotor model against UIUC wind-tunnel data and fitted the motor, ESC and battery model to the manufacturer's bench tables.
  • Ran SU2 RANS CFD to estimate the thrust lost to the arms sitting in the downwash (5.5%) and the airframe drag area.
  • Ran a modal analysis and a Campbell diagram on the printed frame, which flagged a resonance near hover and pointed to a fix.

02Design decisions4 changes

  1. 01

    Blade-pass resonance found by modal analysis

    Finding
    A modal analysis and Campbell diagram of the printed frame put the hover blade-pass frequency (2P, 192 Hz) within 0.1% of a 192 Hz frame mode: the motor pods rocking on the 5 mm pad that is cantilevered past the end of the arm box.
    Action
    Recommended fix: extend the arm box under the motor or thicken the pad, and confirm with a tap test before changing parts, because several arm, pad and leg modes cluster between 185 and 200 Hz.
    Outcome
    Logged as an open design finding; the model has not been changed yet.
  2. 02

    Flight-controller mount close to rotor 1P

    Finding
    The nearest mode to the hover 1P frequency (96 Hz) is the flight controller rocking on its silicone dampers at 103 Hz (+7.4%). With the assumed damper stiffness it would amplify 1P vibration at the IMU about 4.4 times.
    Action
    Recommended softer dampers so every flight-controller mount mode sits below about 35 Hz, plus balanced props; the real damper data is needed to confirm.
  3. 03

    Arm download carried into the performance numbers

    Finding
    SU2 CFD showed the arms and legs under the props cost about 5.5% of rotor thrust in hover, roughly 9% more hover power.
    Action
    Included the loss in the hover power used for the endurance and payload sweeps instead of quoting clean-rotor numbers.
  4. 04

    Motor failure is a documented limit

    Finding
    With a standard PX4-style cascaded controller the simulated vehicle loses attitude control 0.38 s after one motor fails.
    Action
    Recorded as a limit of the design: surviving it needs a fault-tolerant controller or a hexacopter layout, which is not part of this project.

08Limits and assumptionsread before quoting a number

The drone has not been built. Every result is a CAD measurement, a calculation or a computer simulation; none comes from flight or bench tests of this airframe. Damper stiffness, battery resistance and gimbal mass are assumed values, and the calculation report lists each one.
