01Overview

I designed a complete quadcopter airframe in Inventor around a T-Motor AIR2216 / 1045 propulsion set, a Pixhawk 6C flight controller and a 4S 5000 mAh pack. The frame is meant to be printed in PA6-CF on a Bambu printer. Purchased parts are vendor models or sized stand-ins; the printed parts have CAD-measured masses and print files, and the arm and centre frame have FEA results.

The drone has not been built. A later buildability check found three gaps: the parts list has no radio or power distribution board, and the arm as drawn fails the print check. They are listed under design decisions with their fixes.

Tools Autodesk Inventor 2027 / Python / scikit-fem / gmsh / SU2 / Bambu Studio

What I did

  • Wrote a 6-DOF flight simulator in Python (quaternion dynamics, inertia from the CAD, blade-element rotor model) and closed the loop with a PX4-style cascaded PID controller.
  • Checked the rotor model against UIUC wind-tunnel data and fitted the motor, ESC and battery model to the manufacturer's bench tables.
  • Ran SU2 RANS CFD to estimate the thrust lost to the arms sitting in the downwash (5.5%) and the airframe drag area.
  • Ran a modal analysis and a Campbell diagram on the printed frame, which flagged a resonance near hover and pointed to a fix.

02Design decisions5 changes

  1. 01

    Blade-pass resonance found by modal analysis

    Finding
    A modal analysis and Campbell diagram of the printed frame put the hover blade-pass frequency (2P, 192 Hz) within 0.1% of a 192 Hz frame mode: the motor pods rocking on the 5 mm pad that is cantilevered past the end of the arm box.
    Action
    Recommended fix: extend the arm box under the motor or thicken the pad, and confirm with a tap test before changing parts, because several arm, pad and leg modes cluster between 185 and 200 Hz.
    Outcome
    Logged as an open design finding; the model has not been changed yet.
  2. 02

    Flight-controller mount close to rotor 1P

    Finding
    The nearest mode to the hover 1P frequency (96 Hz) is the flight controller rocking on its silicone dampers at 103 Hz (+7.4%). With the assumed damper stiffness it would amplify 1P vibration at the IMU about 4.4 times.
    Action
    Recommended softer dampers so every flight-controller mount mode sits below about 35 Hz, plus balanced props; the real damper data is needed to confirm.
  3. 03

    Arm download carried into the performance numbers

    Finding
    SU2 CFD showed the arms and legs under the props cost about 5.5% of rotor thrust in hover, roughly 9% more hover power.
    Action
    Included the loss in the hover power used for the endurance and payload sweeps instead of quoting clean-rotor numbers.
  4. 04

    Motor failure is a documented limit

    Finding
    With a standard PX4-style cascaded controller the simulated vehicle loses attitude control 0.38 s after one motor fails.
    Action
    Recorded as a limit of the design: surviving it needs a fault-tolerant controller or a hexacopter layout, which is not part of this project.
  5. 05

    Buildability check: radio, power distribution, arm cavities

    Finding
    Checking the BOM and print files against real parts: there is no transmitter or receiver, so the Pixhawk won't arm; nothing feeds the four ESCs, because the PM02 is only a power module; and the arm has 5 sealed cavities with 17-25 mm unsupported roofs (10 mm limit) whose supports could never be removed.
    Action
    Fixes identified: an ELRS radio set (about $90), a power distribution board rated 80 A or more, and solid arm cavities (slicer infill) or 45 deg peaked roofs. The motor bolt pattern (16x19 vs 16x16 mm) and the prop mount still need checking against the real parts.
    Outcome
    Open: the model and BOM have not been changed yet.

P4 What else you need to make it work

From Tests/Buildability_Audit/buildability_audit.md (2026-10-28). Verdict: BUILDABLE WITH FIXES.

Missing from the BOM

  • Optional

    Optional: SiK telemetry radio pair, 915 MHz ~USD 60 (est.)

Change before buying

  1. Arm (QD-103, x4): the hollow box has 5 sealed cavities whose top skin bridges 17-25 mm, so supports can never come out. Delete the cavities and print it solid (about +17 g per arm), or give them 45 deg roofs.
  2. Power module (PM02-V3): it is a power module, not a power distribution board. Keep it for voltage / current sensing and add the PDB above.
  3. Prop lock nut (PROPNUT-M6): T1045 props are sold self-tightening, so the nut is probably not needed. Buy 2 CW-thread and 2 CCW-thread motors to match the props.
  4. Gimbal + camera (GIMBAL-2AX-MICRO) has no real part number (110 g is an estimate). Pick a real unit and re-check the damper plate holes.
  5. Battery: only the 154 x 46 x 30 mm Gens Ace SKU fits the tray, and it ships with Deans / EC5 plugs. Re-terminate it to XT60.
  6. ESC XROTOR-MICRO-30A: no US listing in stock (2026-10-29); an RDQ 30A BLHeli_S DShot600 (RaceDayQuads, $13.49) has the same rating and size class. from BOM price source (QuadDrone_BOM.csv)

Also needed

Measure first
Motor bolt pattern: the CAD uses 16 x 19 mm M3 but T-Motor doesn't publish it (one reseller lists 16 x 16). Measure the motor before printing the arms.
Firmware
PX4 or ArduPilot on the Pixhawk 6C; cap throttle at about 95 % (the motor has 4 % current margin at full throttle).
Printing
PA6-CF needs a hardened 0.4 mm nozzle, an enclosed printer (X1C / P1S, not an A1) and filament dried at 80 C for 8-12 h.
Bench test
Don't run 100 % throttle for more than 60 s on the bench: the PM02 is rated 60 A and full throttle draws 66 A.

Now in the BOM: RC link; Power distribution board, XT60 in + 4 ESC pads, 80 A or more; Wiring; 4S LiPo balance charger, 100 W or more; PA6-CF filament, about 1 kg.

09Limits and assumptionsread before quoting a number

The drone has not been built. Every result is a CAD measurement, a calculation or a computer simulation; none comes from flight or bench tests of this airframe. Damper stiffness, battery resistance and gimbal mass are assumed values, and the calculation report lists each one. The BOM is not complete (no radio or power distribution board), and the arm needs the cavity change above before it can be printed.
