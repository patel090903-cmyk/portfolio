01Overview

A full-size front-left corner for an F1-class single seater (800 kg, 1600 mm track, 305/720 R18 tyre). Everything is driven from one hardpoint table, so the CAD, kinematics, loads and FEA all use the same numbers. 4130 tube wishbones carry GE 12 E spherical bearings; a 7075-T6 upright holds a double-row hub bearing, a carbon disc and a 6-piston caliper; the pushrod runs from the upright to a twin-plate rocker on the chassis that compresses a coil-over damper.

The Inventor assembly uses real joints (revolute pivots, spherical ball joints, a sliding rack) and is driven by two parameters, Heave and Steer. FEA and interference checks found five design problems (for example a pushrod bracket that bent the lower wishbone, and rod ends hitting their clevises at full lock), and I fixed each one in the model.

This is a design study, not a buildable kit. A later check of the bought parts against real catalogue ratings found the rocker bearings, pushrod rod end and spring undersized and the damper not available at the length the CAD needs, and the F1 disc, caliper, rim, tyre and hub bearing can't be bought by an individual. Nothing has been built.

Tools Autodesk Inventor 2027 / Python / SciPy / NumPy / scikit-fem / gmsh / Matplotlib

What I did

  • Solved the corner as a rigid-body mechanism in Python (least squares on the five link closures), modelled the same mechanism in Inventor with real joints, and compared the two solvers at 19 poses: they agree to 1e-5 deg.
  • Optimised the inner tie-rod point to cut bump steer to 0.26 mdeg, and tuned the hardpoints for about 0.5 deg of camber gain per 25 mm, a 72 mm roll centre and 28% anti-dive.
  • Resolved the tyre loads into the six two-force members for 4 g cornering, 4 g braking, combined and 3 g bump. Checked buckling and bolt shear and set the spring rate for a 4 Hz ride; the later catalogue check showed the rocker bearings and spring wire still need upsizing.
  • Ran gmsh + scikit-fem FEA on both wishbones, the upright, the chassis bracket and the rocker with balanced load sets. FEA and interference sweeps drove five redesigns, including moving the pushrod to the upright, turning the lower ball-joint bolt vertical and putting every tube on its force line.

02Design decisions6 changes

  1. 01

    Pushrod moved from the wishbone to the upright

    Finding
    FEA showed the pushrod bracket on the lower wishbone put a couple of about 1.3 kN.m into the 25.4 x 1.65 mm legs, over 1 GPa of stress.
    Action
    Moved the pushrod to a clevis on the upright above the lower ball joint, so all six links are two-force members.
    Outcome
    The wishbone legs carry pure axial load; worst lower wishbone stress is 266 MPa at the 99.9th percentile (SF 1.73).
  2. 02

    Lower ball-joint bolt turned vertical

    Finding
    The 13 kN fore-aft load at the lower ball joint acted axially on a 12 mm housing.
    Action
    Turned the bolt vertical so the load is radial, and widened the apex block in plane.
  3. 03

    Every tube put on its force line

    Finding
    Tube axes sat 10 to 17 mm off the pin-to-pin force lines, which adds bending to members meant to be axial.
    Action
    Routed each wishbone leg on its force line, with plugged ends and deeper necks.
  4. 04

    Moment-balanced upright loads

    Finding
    The first upright load set was not moment-balanced because of the caliper pad radius.
    Action
    Solved the lug forces for force and moment together, then upsized the caliper lugs and the lower arm.
  5. 05

    Clearances at full steering lock

    Finding
    Interference sweeps found rod ends touching their clevises and housings touching spacers at full lock, plus damper perch to rocker, rack clevis to chassis and rim to upper wishbone contacts.
    Action
    Opened the clevis gaps to 24 mm with high-misalignment cone spacers, fixed the other contacts and limited rack travel to ±28 mm.
    Outcome
    0 clashing pairs at 19 poses over travel and full lock.
  6. 06

    Buildability check: bought parts below their loads

    Finding
    Rechecked against real catalogue ratings: the 2x 6202-2RS rocker pivot bearings reach SF 0.37 in the 3 g bump case; the SIKAC 12 M pushrod rod end is really rated 14 kN (SF 0.66), and the BOM lists female rod ends where the CAD needs male; the spring is at SF 0.91 with real Cr-Si wire strength; and the real TTX25 damper is 200 mm long against the 262.5 mm the CAD needs.
    Action
    Fixes identified: 2x GE 15 ES spherical bearings at the rocker and a male M12 rod end rated 42 kN (SF 1.97), plus a new spring and damper. Swapping the F1-only disc, caliper, rim, tyre and 70x110x54 hub bearing for GT3-class parts would mean re-solving the hardpoints.
    Outcome
    Kept as a design study; the model has not been changed.

08Limits and assumptionsread before quoting a number

Designed and simulated only: the corner has not been built or tested, and every number is a model prediction. Loads are quasi-static limit cases from a point-mass vehicle model with assumed downforce, weight distribution and brake bias. Spherical bearings, rod ends, the damper, hub bearing, caliper, disc, rim and tyre are catalogue-dimension or envelope models with estimated masses and assumed ratings; checked against real ratings, several are too small (see the buildability check), so the corner is not buildable as specified. FEA safety factors use the 99.9th-percentile stress because the CAD has sharp corners where real parts would have weld and machining fillets; the report lists the peak values. Heave springs, the anti-roll bar and the right-hand corner are not modelled.
